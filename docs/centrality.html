Central authors in the 2026 co-authorship network

Four ways to rank the authors of the 2026 abstracts by their place in the co-authorship network.

Not in the site menu. The page is public for anyone with the link, but it asks search engines not to index it.

Each author is a point. Two authors are linked when they share at least one abstract, and the link is weighted by how many abstracts they share. The network has 17308 authors. The tables list the 2421 of them with at least one talk; the ranks count everyone.

The four rankings side by side

Each row is an author in the top 25 of at least one ranking. A rank of 1 is the most central on that measure. Sorted by abstracts, which counts every abstract the author is listed on, as first, middle or last author.

Degree

The number of different co-authors. It counts how many people an author has worked with, not how often.

Use it to find the most broadly connected authors. Five co-authors on five abstracts counts the same as five co-authors on one, so repeat collaborations do not add to it.

Strength

The total number of shared abstracts across all co-authors. It is the degree with repeat collaborations counted: an author on 16 abstracts with one group scores high even with few distinct co-authors. The score is that total.

Large author lists, such as consortia, add to it for everyone on them.

PageRank

An author gains weight from co-authors who are themselves well connected, so this ranks authors who sit among other well-connected people, not just people with many links. It is the long-run share of a random walk that moves along shared-abstract links and, 15% of the time, jumps to a random author (the damping factor is 0.85). The score is that share; the values are small because they add up to 1 across all authors.

Betweenness

The share of shortest co-author paths between pairs of authors that pass through this author, with each link counted as one step. High values mark people who connect groups that are otherwise far apart: take them out and many authors are further from each other. A large degree does not guarantee this, and a small group can produce a high value.

The score is estimated from 1,000 starting authors chosen at random, so ranks past the first 20 or so can shift a little between builds.

How to read these

  • Names are matched automatically on first and last name. Two people with the same name are merged, and one person spelled two ways is split.
  • Large author lists link many people. Consortium and biobank papers join everyone on them to each other, which raises all four measures for those authors.
  • Centrality is about who appears on abstracts together. It says nothing about the quality or importance of anyone's work, and it reflects how many abstracts an author submitted.
  • Poster-only authors are not listed, as everywhere on the site, but they still count toward the ranks.
