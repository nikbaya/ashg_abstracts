Analyzing ASHG abstracts, 2022–2026

This site parses the abstracts published for the American Society of Human Genetics annual meeting (2022–2026: 15,327 abstracts) and asks what the field is writing about: which topics and wording are rising or fading, and who presents. Every year ASHG picks a few hundred of its several thousand accepted abstracts for a talk(1,544 over these years); the rest become posters. So it also asks which phrases, institutions and places show up more often among talks.

Read this first. Only accepted abstracts are published, so rejected submissions never appear here, and authors can request a poster instead of a talk, so a poster does not mean an abstract was passed over for a talk. “Talk rate” is the fraction of accepted abstracts presented as a talk, not an acceptance rate or a quality score. Every number here is an association: it says how often a phrase, institution or place goes with a talk, not that it earns one (adding a phrase to your abstract will not make it a talk), and differences can reflect topic, field or what authors chose to submit. Not every affiliation could be matched to an institution or place: see the match rates.

1What's hot at ASHG 2026

Five shifts in what ASHG abstracts mention, 2022–2026. Scroll through them; the chart shows the share of each year's accepted abstracts that mention a topic.

Long reads and proteomics keep growing

long-read is in 8.7% of 2026 abstracts, up from 4.1% in 2022. proteomics rose from 2.5% to 6.4%, and pangenome from 0.3% to 1.7%.

Genomes are pulling ahead of exomes

whole-genome sequencing went from 11.2% of abstracts to 13.8%, while exome sequencing fell from 10.6% to 7.8% (WGS and WES count).

All of Us arrived, and cohorts got more diverse

All of Us went from 1.0% of abstracts to 6.0%, and multi-ancestry from 1.8% to 4.0%. UK Biobank held steady, in 12%–14% of abstracts every year.

New tools caught on within a year

AlphaMissense and gnomAD v4, both released in late 2023, were in 40 and 38 abstracts in 2026. Language models went from 2 abstracts in 2022 to 49.

COVID-19 faded

COVID-19 (or SARS-CoV-2) was in 2.7% of 2022 abstracts and 0.8% of 2026's.

42026 abstracts are worded differently

“framework” is in 27% of 2026 abstracts, up from 10% in 2022–2025. It is one of 288 words significantly more common in 2026, and most of them are general wording rather than new topics.

How this differs from the sections above. Those look at hand-picked or talk-leaning phrases. This one tests every single word, comparing 2026 with 2022–2025, whatever the topic and whether it was a talk or a poster.
