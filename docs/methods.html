Methods and caveats

What the numbers mean

  • Accepted abstracts only. The booklets list abstracts that were accepted, and authors can ask for a poster. “Talk rate” is the fraction of accepted abstracts presented as a talk (platform or plenary); it is not an acceptance rate or a quality score.
  • Associations, not causes. A phrase that is common in talks may simply mark a field or a large consortium that tends to get talks; it does not make an abstract more likely to be picked.
  • Small numbers are noisy. Every share comes with a 95% interval; wide intervals mean little can be said.
  • What is not shown. The site never names an institution, country, metro area or person for having no talks: those rows are left out of the tables and figures here. World regions are shown in full. The complete aggregate tables are in the repository.

How it's built

  • Parsing. Text is extracted with PyMuPDF and split into abstracts using each booklet's layout (session headers, titles, author blocks, “Abstract:” labels). Every parsed abstract is checked against the booklet's table of contents, and a hand-checked sample is used as a test set. Abstracts cut off mid-text in a booklet count toward totals and first-author credit, but not senior-author credit or the text analyses.
  • Institutions. A hand-curated list of 211 institutions and their spelling variants. Schools fold into their university (except Harvard Medical School); NIH institutes fold into NIH; hospitals and independent institutes stay separate.
  • Countries and regions. Each author's country comes from the location at the end of their affiliations (“Seoul, Korea, Republic of”; “Boston, MA”), which nearly every affiliation in 2022–2024 has. When there is none (most of 2025), it comes from the resolved institution, else from country, demonym or city names in the affiliation (“Seoul National University”), else from a hand-checked list of the remaining affiliation strings (mostly companies and smaller institutes; LLM-labeled, then spot-checked). Countries are grouped into UN M49 regions, with the Americas split into Northern America and Latin America and the Caribbean. An author with affiliations in several countries counts in full for each, so an abstract can count for several countries (once per region), and rows can add up to more than the total. Because the first source that finds a country is used, an affiliation without its own location can be missed when another one has it; nearly every 2022–2024 affiliation gives a location, so this is rare there. Every 2025 first- and senior-author affiliation was reviewed (LLM pass, each correction checked by hand), and those hand-checked countries take precedence. “Hong Kong, China” counts as Hong Kong only.
  • Metro areas. Each institution is mapped by hand to a metro (US Census metropolitan areas, with San Francisco + San Jose combined as the SF Bay Area and Durham–Chapel Hill + Raleigh as the Research Triangle). Institutions with sites in several metros are left out.
  • Senior authors. The last listed person (consortium names skipped). Within a year, the same person = same first + last name, and for common surnames (seen with 5+ different first names) also a shared institution. Across years the 2022–2024 booklets give only initials, so the same person = same first initial + last name; surnames seen with 5+ different initials need a shared institution, and two different spelled-out first names (2025) are never merged unless one is a short form of the other (Alex, Alexander).
  • Phrases. 1–3-word n-grams from the abstract text, appearing in at least 5 talks (or the equivalent share of posters). Each gets a talk-vs-poster odds ratio with +0.5 smoothing and a 95% CI, ranked by z-score. Phrases that share a word or are an acronym of each other and appear in mostly the same abstracts (Jaccard ≥ 0.45; exact acronyms like “QTLs” always) form one group, shown under its best-ranked member.
  • All years. Institution, place and author talk rates pool the counts from every year. Phrase odds ratios across years are Mantel–Haenszel estimates stratified by year (each year's 2×2 table, +0.5 in every cell, Robins–Breslow–Greenland variance), because the baseline talk rate differs by year.
  • Intervals. Talk rates use 95% Wilson intervals.
